How to Use the Plagiarism Checker

  1. Paste your draft into the "Your text" box.
  2. Paste the source material you want to compare against into one, two or three comparison boxes — for example the original article you summarised.
  3. Click "Check for Duplicates". Each sentence is compared, and exact or near-exact matches are flagged.
  4. Review the highlighted duplicate sentences and rewrite them before publishing.

Why Check for Duplicates?

Accidental overlap happens more often than people think: a copied phrase from research notes, a product description reused across pages, or a paraphrase that stayed too close to the source. Search engines and universities both take originality seriously, and catching duplicates early is far less painful than discovering them after publication.

  • Sentence-level analysis — each sentence is checked individually, not just the whole document.
  • Catches near-duplicates — sentences sharing 80% or more of their words are flagged too.
  • Compare up to 3 sources — useful when several references fed one draft.
  • Clear verdict — an originality score plus a highlighted list of every matched sentence.
  • Honest by design — this tool compares against texts you supply; it does not crawl the internet.
  • 100% private — analysis runs on your device; nothing is uploaded.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this plagiarism checker really free?

Yes. It is completely free with no sign-up and no limits on how often you use it.

Does it search the internet for matches?

No — and that is deliberate. This tool compares your text against source texts you paste in yourself, which keeps your draft private. For web-wide checks, use a dedicated online service in addition to this one.

What counts as a duplicate sentence?

A sentence counts as duplicate if it exactly matches a sentence in any comparison text, or if at least 80% of its words appear in a comparison sentence.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. All comparison happens with JavaScript in your own browser, so your text never leaves your device.
