Tips for Web-Ready Images

  • Compress before uploading — aim for under 200 KB per image on content pages; the compressor shows exactly how much you save.
  • Prefer WebP for photos — it typically delivers the same visual quality as JPEG at roughly half the file size, and all modern browsers support it.
  • Resize to display size — uploading a 4000px photo that displays at 800px wastes bandwidth; resize first, then compress.
  • Use PNG only when needed — PNG is ideal for logos and graphics with transparency, but wasteful for photographs.
  • Don't forget the favicon — generate the full 16/32/48/180px set so your site icon looks sharp in tabs, bookmarks and on mobile home screens.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these image tools really free?

Yes — all four image tools are free with no sign-up, no watermarks and no usage limits.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Compression, conversion and resizing all happen locally in your browser via the canvas API. Your files never leave your device.

Which format should I use for my website?

WebP is the best default for photos, JPEG is a safe fallback, and PNG is best for logos or anything needing transparency.

Will compression ruin my image quality?

At the default quality setting the difference is virtually invisible. Lower the quality slider gradually and compare the preview until you find your sweet spot.
