Where Can You Use QR Codes?

QR codes bridge the gap between the physical and digital worlds: one scan takes a phone straight to your page, menu, app or contact card. They work on packaging, business cards, posters, receipts and presentations — anywhere typing a long URL would be annoying.

  • Custom colors — match the code to your brand instead of plain black.
  • Three sizes — from small web icons to crisp print-ready images.
  • High error correction — codes stay scannable even if partly damaged or obscured.
  • Instant PNG download — no account, no watermark, ready to use.
  • Free forever — generate as many codes as you like.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do these QR codes expire?

No. The code simply encodes the text or link you entered, so it works forever — as long as the link itself stays live.

Can I track scans of my QR code?

Not with a static code like this one. If you need scan analytics, use a dynamic QR service; for simple linking, static codes are faster and free.

Will my custom colors still scan?

Yes, as long as there is good contrast between the dark and light colors. Avoid very light dark-colors or very dark light-colors, and always test-scan before printing.

What can I put in a QR code?

Website URLs, plain text, phone numbers, email addresses, Wi-Fi credentials and more. Keep it short — shorter content makes denser, easier-to-scan codes.
